# Storefront API (v1)

The storefront is backed by Supabase Postgres. Every read and write below
runs against the real database. There are no mocks or fixtures. Business rules
live in the database, where both the web app and API clients hit them: stock
reservation, totals, one review per customer, verified purchases, and the address
book limits. They are enforced by row-level security, triggers and
`SECURITY DEFINER` functions. The web app (server actions) and this HTTP API
are two thin clients over the same data layer (`lib/data/*`).

Base URL: `https://<host>/api/v1` (local: `http://localhost:3000/api/v1`).
`GET /api/v1` returns a machine-readable index of what follows.

## Conventions

| Topic | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Format | JSON in, JSON out. `POST`/`PATCH` bodies must be `Content-Type: application/json` (else `415`). An empty body is treated as `{}`. |
| Store | `?market=US` (amazon.com, USD) or `?market=IN` (amazon.in, INR). You can also send an `X-Market` header. The default is `US`. Carts, orders, addresses, categories and search are all per store. |
| Auth | `Authorization: Bearer <accessToken>` from `POST /auth/token`. The web app's session cookie is accepted too. Endpoints marked 🔒 return `401 not_authenticated` without one. |
| Guest cart | Guests address their cart with `X-Cart-Token: <uuid>`. You don't create one: the first cart write without a token mints one. It comes back in the `X-Cart-Token` response header and as `cartToken` in the body. Keep it, then fold it into the account after sign-in with `POST /cart/merge`. |
| Money | Integer minor units (cents / paise), e.g. `priceMinor: 2199` = $21.99. The currency is on the cart / order / product (`curBase`). |
| Errors | `{"error": {"code", "message", "detail?"}}` with a matching status (table below). `detail` names the offending field for `422`s. |
| CORS | `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. The allowed headers are `Authorization, Content-Type, X-Cart-Token, X-Market`. `X-Cart-Token` is exposed. |
| Caching | Every response is `Cache-Control: no-store`. |

### Store rules (from the `markets` table)

| | US | IN |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Currency | USD | INR |
| Tax | 8% added at checkout | prices include GST |
| Shipping | $5.99, free from $35 | ₹40, free from ₹499 |
| Faster delivery | $9.99 | ₹99 |
| Payment methods | `card`, `giftcard` | `upi`, `card`, `netbanking`, `cod`, `emi`, `amazonpay`, `paylater` |
| Returns | 30 days after delivery | 10 days after delivery |
| Address | US ZIP, 2-letter state | 6-digit pincode, `line2` (area) required, optional `landmark`, `addressType: home|office` |

Max 30 units per cart line. Quantities are also capped at available stock.

Plus members (see `/me/plus`) get standard delivery free on every order, whatever the total, and faster delivery free too. A membership is on one of its store's plans (monthly or annual, and 3 months on amazon.in) and renews at the end of each period; members can switch plans from the next renewal or turn renewal off, keeping the benefits until the period ends. The cart and order totals already reflect this for a signed-in member. In the US store a member can also pick a Delivery Day (Amazon Day), a day of the week to get orders on together; see `speed: "day"` under `POST /orders`.

Plus Household, as on Amazon Household: a member shares their delivery benefits (FREE delivery on every order and FREE faster delivery) with one other adult, at no extra cost (`/me/plus/household`). The member invites them by email; the invite waits until that adult, signed in with that email, accepts it (`POST /me/plus/household/invites/:owner`) or declines it. From then on they're priced as a member in both stores, and `/me/plus` returns the membership they share with `shared: {ownerName?}` (its plan and renewal are the member's; they can't change them, nor pick a Delivery Day). Either can end the sharing at any time (`DELETE /me/plus/household`), and it ends with the membership. Someone with their own Plus can't join a household (`409 plus_owned`), and joining Plus takes them out of the one they're in.

Paying with the store balance (`giftcard` in the US, `amazonpay` in India) takes the order total from the shopper's gift card balance in that store when the order is placed. It fails with `409 insufficient_balance` when the balance doesn't cover it, and the cart is kept. Redeem gift card codes into the balance with `/me/balance/redeem`; each account can get one demo gift card per store (US $100, India ₹5,000). Refunds of balance orders (a cancel, or a received return) go back to the balance.

When the balance doesn't cover the order, part of it can still come from there (a split payment, as Amazon's "Use your gift card balance" with a card for the rest): `POST /orders` with `useBalance: true` and `paymentMethod` `card`, `upi` or `netbanking` takes the whole balance when the order is placed and charges the rest to the method (a card order's Stripe Checkout asks for that part only). The order says so in `split: {balanceMinor, chargedMinor}`. A balance that covers the whole order is `409 balance_covers_order` (pay with the balance method instead), and `useBalance` with any other method is `422 invalid_input` (`detail` `useBalance`). An unpaid card order that's abandoned, lapses or is cancelled gives its balance part back; if its payment lands afterwards it's taken again, or, when the balance has been spent meanwhile, the order stays cancelled and the card is refunded (`balance_spent`). Refunds of a split order (cancelling it or some of its items, a return refunded to how they paid) go back to the method first, up to `chargedMinor`, and the rest to the balance at once: `balanceMinor` on the refund (`balanceRefundMinor` on a return) is that part, and a refund that's all balance is `succeeded` straight away.

Pay Later (India), as amazon.in's Amazon Pay Later: a shopper activates it at once (`POST /me/pay-later`; a demo, with no credit check) and gets the store's limit (`markets.pay_later_limit_minor`: ₹60,000). `POST /orders` with `paymentMethod: "paylater"` then buys on credit: the order is placed at once, labelled `Pay Later`, and uses that much of the limit. It fails with `409 pay_later_inactive` before activating, `409 pay_later_overdue` while a bill is overdue, and `409 pay_later_limit` (`detail`: what's available) when the available limit doesn't cover it; the cart is kept each time. It can't be split with the balance. Its refunds (a cancelled order or items, a received return) go back to Pay Later at once and free up the limit; a return can't be refunded to the balance instead (`422 invalid_input`, `detail` `refundTo`). Everything bought before the 1st of the month (store time), less its refunds, that repayments haven't covered is that month's bill, due on the 5th (`GET /me/pay-later`); repay any time with `POST /me/pay-later/repayments`, which pays off the oldest purchases first.

Mobile recharge (India), as amazon.in's Amazon Pay recharges (a demo: no operator is reached, nothing is recharged or charged): pick a prepaid plan from `GET /recharge-plans` and `POST /me/recharges` with the number, its circle and `method` `amazonpay` (from the store balance, `409 insufficient_balance` when it doesn't cover the plan), `upi` or `netbanking`. Every recharge pays 2% of the plan back into the balance at once (rounded down to the rupee, up to ₹25) as a `cashback` entry; one paid from the balance is a `recharge` entry. Recharges show in `GET /me/transactions` as `source: "recharge"` charges with the `number`.

Bill payments (India), as amazon.in's Amazon Pay "Pay bills" (a demo: no biller is reached, and the bills are made up): `GET /billers` lists who can be paid — electricity, DTH, broadband, piped gas, water and FASTag — with what each calls the account and what one looks like. A biller that `fetches` sends a bill: `GET /billers/:id/bill?account=` shows this month's (the same every time for an account, due on the 20th), and `POST /me/bill-payments` pays it in full, once (`409 bill_paid` after that; `409 amount_mismatch` when the amount isn't the bill's). DTH and FASTag take any whole-rupee amount within the biller's limits. Pay with `amazonpay` (from the store balance, a `bill` entry; `409 insufficient_balance` when it doesn't cover it), `upi` or `netbanking`. Payments show in `GET /me/transactions` as `source: "bill"` charges with the `biller`.

Trade-In (US), as amazon.com's Trade-In (a demo: nothing is shipped and no label is printed): `GET /exchange-devices` lists the phones and laptops the store takes, and `POST /me/trade-ins` with one of them and its `condition` (`good`, or `screen_damaged` for half the value) trades it in for that quote. The trade-in has a prepaid label code (`shipCode`) and 7 days to send it (`shipBy`); it can be cancelled until it arrives (`POST /me/trade-ins/:id/cancel`). When the store receives it, an admin marks the condition it came in and the shopper's balance gets what it's worth in that condition, never more than the quote, as a `trade_in` entry; one that isn't the device, or doesn't switch on, is sent back with a note. A shopper can have 5 waiting to be sent (`409 trade_in_limit`). India takes old devices only in exchange on a new one (*Exchange offers*), so it has no Trade-In (`404`).

Gift cards can also be bought, by card only: `POST /me/gift-cards` records the purchase and returns a Stripe Checkout URL for exactly its amount (whole currency units, US $1–$2,000, India ₹10–₹10,000). Once Stripe reports the session paid (the `/gift-cards/success` return trip or the webhook, both re-reading the session from Stripe), a service-role call checks the amount and currency and issues a new code. The buyer sees it in `GET /me/gift-cards` and can give it away or redeem it themselves. No email is sent.

The balance can also be reloaded by card, as Amazon's "Reload Your Balance" (amazon.in's "Add Money"): `POST /me/balance/reload` works the same way, for the same amounts. The paid amount goes straight onto the balance as a `reload` entry, with no code.

Cards can be saved, as Amazon's "Your Payments": they're kept on Stripe, never here. A signed-in shopper paying by card (an order, a gift card or a reload) gets Stripe's page with their saved cards and the option to save the one they type. `GET /me/cards` lists them, `POST /me/cards` opens Stripe's page to add one, and `DELETE /me/cards/:id` removes one. The web app has them at `/account/payments`.

Subscribe & Save, as on Amazon: a product with `subscribeSave: true` (skin care, here) can be subscribed to, `qty` (1–10) every `everyMonths` (1–6) months (`/me/subscriptions`). Each delivery is an ordinary order (`from_cart` false) at 5% off each item, 15% when 5 or more of the shopper's subscriptions arrive in the same delivery (same day, address and payment method), with free delivery and the store's usual tax. Subscribing places the first delivery straight away; after that the store places each one itself (hourly, by the store's own date), then moves `nextOn` on. Nobody is at the checkout then, so deliveries are only paid with what the store can charge by itself: the store balance (US `giftcard`, India `amazonpay`) or India's `upi` and `netbanking`; not cards. A delivery that can't go (out of stock, off sale, no address, the balance short) is skipped, and its subscriptions carry the `issue` until the next one goes.

Lightning Deals, as on Amazon: a product's price drops for a few hours, for so many units. The store runs them itself: each hour it plans two per store, starting two hours later and lasting six, 15–40% off (whole rupees in India) for a third of the stock (5–100 units); each minute it starts the ones due and ends the ones over. While a deal is live the product itself carries the deal price, with `deal: true` and what it cost before as its struck `listMinor` (or its list price, when that's higher), so carts, checkout, coupons and search price it as they price anything else (a cart line added before it shows the change). Every unit ordered at the deal price is claimed against it; when the last one is, it ends early and the price goes back. It also ends early if the product goes off sale or runs out of stock, or if an admin changes its price (theirs stands). `GET /deals/lightning` lists a store's live and upcoming deals, and `/products/:id` has the product's `lightningDeal`. Admins schedule deals of their own too, and cancel any (`/admin/lightning-deals`). Signed-in shoppers can watch an upcoming deal (`/deals/lightning/:id/watch`), and when it goes live it's in their messages (`deal_live`); `/deals/lightning/watched` lists the ones they watch. Plus members (and their household) get early access, as Prime members do: from `earlyAccessAt`, half an hour before a deal starts, they pay its price already. The product keeps its own price until then, so their cart, quotes and orders take the difference off as a member saving (the line's `earlyAccessMinor`, inside `memberMinor`), before a member deal's percent on what's left and anything else. What they order is claimed against the deal, so one can sell out before it starts (and then never does); early access needs units left, and isn't open while another of the product's deals is live.

Some products have a coupon, a percent off (5–50%). A signed-in shopper applies it with `POST /products/:id/coupon`; while it's applied, the percent comes off every unit of that product in their cart and orders in that store, rounded to the minor unit per unit. A coupon isn't used up by an order: it stays applied until the shopper removes it. Delivery's free threshold and the tax are worked out on the subtotal after coupons. Guest carts never get coupon discounts.

## Auth

Every token pair is `{tokenType, accessToken, refreshToken, expiresAt, expiresIn, user: {id, email}, twoStepRequired}`.

Two-step verification, as Amazon's: a shopper can add an authenticator app (TOTP codes) to their password (`/me/two-step`, or Login & security on the web). With it on, `/auth/token` returns a token pair with `twoStepRequired: true` that has only passed the password: every other call refuses it with `401 two_step_required` (the database refuses it too, so it can't be used against Supabase directly either). Send the app's 6-digit code to `/auth/token/verify` with that token for a full one. Turning it on asks for a code anywhere else the account is signed in. On the web, signing in (or opening a password reset link) asks for the code at `/signin/verify` before anything else.

Token pair: `{tokenType: "bearer", accessToken, refreshToken, expiresAt, expiresIn, user: {id, email}}`.

`PATCH /me`:
- Changing `email` or setting `newPassword` needs `currentPassword` (`422 invalid_input`, `detail: "currentPassword"` when it's wrong). A taken email is `409 duplicate`.
- Every field is checked before anything changes: name 1–80 characters, password 6–72.
- A new password ends every session of the account, including the caller's, so switch to the returned `session`.
- Password resets are web only: `/signin/forgot` emails a link that opens `/auth/confirm` and then the Login & security page.
Access tokens are Supabase JWTs (1 h by default). The API only uses them to call
Postgres as that user, so RLS decides what each caller can see.

`Product` has these fields:
- Identity: `id, market, title, brand?, category, categoryName, image`
- Price: `priceMinor, listMinor?, dealPct?, deal?, curBase`
- Ratings: `rating, reviewCount`
- Fulfilment: `seller, shipsFrom, stock, maxPerCustomer?`. `maxPerCustomer` is the product's "Limit N per customer", when it has one (see *Purchase limits* under Orders)
- Content: `bullets[], badge?, boughtPastMonth?`
- Variant: `variant?: {group, axis, label}` when the product is one option of a variant group (`/products/:id` lists the others)
- Unit price: `unit?: {qty, kind}` says how much a product sold by measure holds (`{qty: 3, kind: "fl_oz"}`, `{qty: 150, kind: "ml"}`, `{qty: 30, kind: "count"}`); `kind` is one of `count`, `oz`, `fl_oz`, `lb`, `g`, `kg`, `ml`, `l` and `qty` has up to 2 decimals. The storefront shows the price per unit beside the price, as Amazon does: per one count, ounce, fluid ounce, pound, kilogram or litre, or per 100 g / 100 ml (`$58.99 ($19.66 / Fl Oz)`, `₹178 (₹118.67 / 100 ml)`)
- Quantity discount: `qtyDiscount?: {percentOff, minQty}` is "Save 5% when you buy 2 or more": `percentOff` (1–50) comes off each unit of a cart line or order item of at least `minQty` (2–99) of the product. It's worked out after the coupon, rounded down to the minor unit, and goes into the line's `discountMinor` (see the cart's `qtyDiscountMinor`); a promotion code comes off what's left after it
- Plus exclusive deal: `memberPct?` (1–50) is a member price, "Plus exclusive deal": a Plus member (or someone in their household) pays `memberPct`% less on each unit, rounded down to the minor unit. It's the first thing off the price, before a coupon, quantity discount, promotion code and Bank Offer (each of those works on what's left), and goes into the line's `discountMinor` (see the cart's `memberMinor`). Shoppers who aren't members see what members pay
- Sizes: `sizes?: string[]` for clothes and shoes that come in sizes (`["7", "7.5", …]`, `["UK 6", …]`, `["S", "M", …]`), in the order the size chart lists them. One of them is picked before it goes in the cart or is bought with Buy Now; all sizes share the product's `stock`
- Pre-order: `releaseAt?` is when a product not out yet is released (an admin sets the day; it's that day's midnight in the store). Until then it's sold as a pre-order: the storefront says when it's released and its Buy Now reads "Pre-order now", and orders of it ship on the day (see *Pre-orders* under Orders). Once the date has passed it's an ordinary product
- Climate Pledge Friendly: `climate?: string[]`, the product's sustainability certifications, any of `compact` (Compact by Design), `carbon` (Carbon reduced), `recycled` (Recycled materials), `organic` (Organic content), `energy` (Energy efficient), `forest` (Responsibly sourced wood and paper) and `safer` (Safer chemicals), in that order; absent when it has none. They're the store's own, a demo's. Another seller's offer has its product's
- Small Business: `smallBusiness?: true` when the product's brand is one this store marks as a small business (`GET /small-businesses` says what it makes; the storefront shows it on the product's page and brand store). Another seller's offer goes by its product's brand
- Subscribe & Save: `subscribeSave?: true` when the product can be subscribed to (`POST /me/subscriptions`). Another seller's offer, and a product limited per customer, can't be
- Other sellers: `offerOf?` is set on another seller's offer on a product: the product's id. An offer is bought like any product (its own id goes in the cart and on orders) and shares the product's title, image, category, reviews and `rating`, with its own price, stock, seller and `condition?`: `renewed`, `used_like_new`, `used_very_good`, `used_good` or `used_acceptable` (absent when new), with the seller's `conditionNote?` ("Minor scuffs on the case"). Offers never appear in search, browse, charts or recommendations (search's `condition` finds the products they're offers of); `/products/:id/offers` lists a product's, and the storefront shows them on its page ("Other sellers on Amazon") and at `/product/:id/offers` ("Buying options"). An offer's page is its product's. An order item bought from one has `offerOf` and `condition` too
- Renewed: `GET /renewed` lists a store's renewed offers (the storefront's `/renewed`). In a store with the Renewed Guarantee (the US, 90 days; none in India) an item bought renewed can be returned within that many days of delivery, or its category's window if longer, for a refund or a replacement, whatever its category's rules: the order item's `returnDays` says so. Elsewhere it has its category's window like anything else. The storefront words it "90-day Renewed Guarantee" on the offer, as this store's own, for demonstration
- Status: `archived?`, true when an admin has taken it off sale. Archived products never appear in search and browse (`/products`), deals or compare. Their page and reviews stay, and carts and collections that already hold one keep it.

## Cart

Every cart response is `{cart}`, where `Cart` has these fields:
- Store: `market, currency, freeShipThresholdMinor`
- Lines: `count, selectedCount, lines: [{product, qty, lineTotalMinor, inStock, available, coupon, discountMinor, memberMinor?, earlyAccessMinor?, qtyDiscountMinor?, selected, protection?, addedPriceMinor?, size?, needsSize?}]`. `available` is false for an archived product. Such a line can only be removed, and `inStock` is false for it too. `coupon` is null or `{percentOff, clipped}`; `discountMinor` is what the applied coupon takes off the line (`lineTotalMinor` is before it).
- Selection: each line is ticked for checkout (`selected`, true when added). `count` is every item in the cart; `selectedCount` and the totals cover the ticked lines only. Checkout orders the ticked lines and leaves the rest in the cart. Adding a product (`POST /cart/items`) ticks its line again.
- Price changes: `addedPriceMinor` is the product's price when the line went into the cart (kept when a guest cart merges on sign-in, and absent on Buy Now's quote). When it differs from `product.priceMinor`, the price has changed since, as the web cart's "Important messages about items in your cart" says.
- Protection plans: a line whose product the store covers has `protection: {unitMinor, added}` (absent otherwise). The plan costs about a tenth of the price per unit, rounded to $X.99 / ₹X99, for electronics and computers from $25 in the US, and mobiles, electronics, wearables, computers and kitchen appliances from ₹1,000 in India. Add or drop it with `PATCH /cart/items/:productId {protection}`; it stays on when a guest cart merges on sign-in.
- Sizes: a line of a product with `sizes` has the one picked as `size`. A cart holds one size of a product at a time; change it with `PATCH /cart/items/:productId {size}`. `needsSize: true` when the line has no size, or one the product no longer comes in (an admin changed its sizes): it can't be checked out until one is picked (`POST /orders` fails with `422 size_required`, `detail` the product's id).
- Totals: `{subtotalMinor, discountMinor, memberMinor?, qtyDiscountMinor?, shipMinor, taxMinor, protectionMinor?, totalMinor}`, where `totalMinor = subtotalMinor - discountMinor + shipMinor + taxMinor + protectionMinor`. `protectionMinor` is the plans on the ticked lines, untaxed and outside free delivery (absent with none)
- Quantity discounts: a line of at least a product's `qtyDiscount.minQty` has `qtyDiscountMinor`, the quantity discount's part of its `discountMinor` (absent otherwise), and the totals add them up as `qtyDiscountMinor` (inside `discountMinor`)
- Plus exclusive deals: when the shopper is a Plus member, a line of a product with `memberPct` has `memberMinor`, the member price's part of its `discountMinor` (absent otherwise), and the totals add them up as `memberMinor` (inside `discountMinor`). A member's line of a product in a Lightning Deal's early access has `earlyAccessMinor`, its price down to the deal's (inside `memberMinor`; see `/deals/lightning`)
- Promotions: only a checkout quote (`GET /orders/quote`) has them. The code's part of each line's `discountMinor` is the line's `promoMinor` (absent when it took nothing off), the totals add `promoMinor` (inside `discountMinor`), and the cart names the code as `promo: {code, percentOff, description, category?}`.

Prices and totals are computed by the database on every read.

The web app offers it on the order page while the order is on its way. |

There is no guest checkout. Orders belong to an account, so every route here needs a signed-in user (`401 not_authenticated`), and so does `place_order()` in the database. A guest's cart carries over: sign in, then `POST /cart/merge`.

How `POST /orders` works:
- In one transaction, it validates the address for the store, locks the products and reserves stock (`409 insufficient_stock`). A cart holding an archived product fails with `409 product_unavailable` (`detail` is its id) until that line is removed. It then snapshots each line's title, price, seller and coupon discount and computes the totals. An order's `totals` carry `discountMinor`, and each item `unitDiscountMinor` when a coupon applied; a return refunds what was paid for an item, after its coupon.
- **Ticked lines only:** a cart checkout orders the ticked lines (`selected`). The unticked ones stay in the cart. `409 nothing_selected` when the cart has lines but none is ticked.
- **Non-card methods** return `201 {order}` with `status: "placed"`, and the ordered lines leave the cart.
- **`card`** returns `201 {order, checkoutUrl}` with `status: "awaiting_payment"`. Send the customer to `checkoutUrl`, a Stripe-hosted page (test mode: card `4242 4242 4242 4242`). The cart is kept until payment succeeds.
- **Pickup:** `shipping: {fullName, phone, pickupPoint}` has the order delivered to a Hub Locker or Hub Counter (`GET /pickup-points`) instead of an address, as Amazon Hub does: the point's name and street become `shipTo.line1` and `line2` (its city, state and postcode the rest), the name and phone are who collects it, and there are no delivery instructions. It runs on the usual schedule (any speed); once delivered it's ready for pickup, and the order's `pickup.code` (six digits) opens the locker or is given at the counter, for the point's `holdDays`. `404 pickup_point_not_found` for a point not in this store or not taking orders; a locker with `paymentMethod: "cod"` is `422 pickup_cod_unavailable`. Sending the order to a saved address (`PATCH /orders/:id {addressId}`) ends the pickup; changing its instructions is `409 order_not_editable`.
- **Delivery speed:** `speed: "fast"` ships within 3 hours and delivers on the evening run (out at 17:00, delivered by 19:30 store time): the same day for orders placed by noon, otherwise the next day. It's offered only when it arrives before standard delivery would; at other times, or for an unknown speed, the order fails with `422 delivery_option_unavailable`. The store's fast fee (`markets.fast_ship_fee_minor`: $9.99 / ₹99) replaces the delivery charge; it's free only for Plus members. `speed: "day"` sends the order to the member's Delivery Day (US store, Plus members who've picked one with `PATCH /me/plus`): it arrives on the first such weekday on or after the day standard delivery would, on the morning run (out at 9:00, delivered by 11:30 store time), and ships at 18:00 the evening before (or when standard would, if that's later). It's priced like standard delivery, so FREE for members. Without Plus, without a Delivery Day, or in the India store it fails with `422 delivery_option_unavailable`. `speed: "no_rush"` (No-Rush Shipping, US store) ships 4 days after standard would, priced like standard delivery, and earns the store's reward (`markets.no_rush_reward_minor`: $1.00), kept on the order as `noRushReward: {amountMinor, creditedAt?}`: it's added to the gift card balance once the order has shipped (when the balance is next read, or spent at checkout), and a cancelled order earns nothing. In the India store it fails with `422 delivery_option_unavailable`.
- **Pre-orders:** an order holding a product whose `releaseAt` is still to come is a pre-order: it takes the latest such release as its own `releaseAt` and its schedule runs from then rather than from when it was placed (it ships 10 hours into the release day), with everything else on it waiting too, so it can be cancelled right up until then. When an admin moves the release the orders waiting on it move with it (clearing it ships them now), and cancelling the pre-ordered items (`/orders/:id/cancel-items`) lets the rest go sooner
- **Pre-order Price Guarantee:** as on Amazon, a pre-order pays the lowest price its item sells for between the order and the end of its release day (in the store's time zone). When an admin lowers the price (not a Lightning Deal), each placed order that pre-ordered it and isn't delivered gets the difference: the item's `unitGuaranteeMinor` (part of its `unitDiscountMinor`, added up in `totals.guaranteeMinor`) goes up by it, the order is repriced as cancelling items reprices it (the tax only goes down, delivery stays), and the difference with its tax is refunded like cancelled items are, as a `cancellations` entry with `priceGuarantee` and no `items`. The guarantee never takes an item below nothing, and later drops pay only what's beyond the earlier ones
- **Protection plans:** each cart line with its plan on (or `buyNow.protection: true`) buys the store's plan per unit with the item: the item has `protectionMinor` (per unit) and the order `totals.protectionMinor`, untaxed and outside free delivery. A line whose product is no longer covered goes without. Cancelling an item before it ships refunds its plan, and so does returning it for a refund (a replacement keeps the plan).
- **EMI** (India): `paymentMethod: "emi"` is for orders of ₹3,000 or more (`markets.emi_min_minor`); below that, and in the US store, the order fails with `422 emi_unavailable` (US: `payment_method_unavailable`). `emiMonths` is 3, 6, 9 or 12 (3 when left out; `422 invalid_input`, `detail` `emiMonths`, otherwise) and is ignored for other methods. The order keeps it as `emiMonths`, and `paymentLabel` reads `EMI · 6 months`.
- **Pay on Delivery** (India): `paymentMethod: "cod"` is for orders of up to ₹50,000 (`markets.cod_max_minor`, on the order's total), as on amazon.in; over that the order fails with `422 cod_unavailable` (the cart is kept) and can be paid another way. Checkout shows the method greyed out with why.
- **Delivery OTP:** an order whose total reaches the store's threshold (`markets.delivery_otp_min_minor`: India ₹10,000, US $500) gets a six-digit `deliveryOtp`, as Amazon asks for one at the door on high-value orders: the shopper reads it out to the delivery associate, who hands the order over only then. The order page shows it on the delivery day (out for delivery), and Your Orders beside the order while it's out. A pickup order has its pickup code instead; sending one to an address (`PATCH /orders/:id {addressId}`) gives it an OTP when it's over the threshold.
- **Buy Now:** send `buyNow: {productId, qty}` to order just that product (`qty` 1 up to the store's line limit) instead of the cart. The cart isn't needed and is left as it is, whatever the payment method; `404 product_not_found` if the product isn't in this store. A card order's cancel page returns to that product's checkout. A product with `sizes` needs `buyNow.size`, one of them (`422 size_required`, `detail` its id).
- **Gifts:** send `gift: {message?}` (or `gift: true`) to mark the order as a gift. The note is trimmed and can be up to 240 characters (`422 invalid_input` beyond that); a blank one means no note. Add `wrap: true` to gift-wrap every item at the store's fee per item (US $3.99, India ₹30): it's `totals.wrapMinor`, untaxed and outside free delivery, and the order shows `gift.wrapped: true`. `422 invalid_input` (`detail` `gift_wrap`) for wrap without a gift.
- **Promotion codes:** send `promoCode` to take a store promotion off the order, as Amazon's "Add a gift card or promotion code" does. A code is a percent off (1–50%) either the whole order or one category's items, worked out per unit after the coupon and any quantity discount and rounded down to the minor unit, so it goes into each item's `unitDiscountMinor` (the order's `discountMinor`) and returns and cancellations refund what was paid after it. The order has `promoCode`, each item it took money off `unitPromoMinor` (its part of `unitDiscountMinor`) and the totals `promoMinor`. Delivery's free threshold and the tax are worked out after it. A code can have a minimum spend on its qualifying items (after coupons and quantity discounts, ticked lines only) and is good for one order per customer, which a cancelled order gives back. Codes are case-insensitive. The order fails with `422 promo_invalid` for a code the store doesn't have, `422 promo_expired` for one that has ended or not started, `409 promo_used` once you've used it, `422 promo_not_eligible` when nothing in the order qualifies, and `422 promo_min_spend` (`detail` the minimum, in minor units) below its minimum. `GET /coupons` lists them; `GET /orders/quote` prices one before ordering.
- **Bank Offers:** with `paymentMethod` `netbanking` or `emi`, send `bank` (the shopper's bank, as named in `GET /bank-offers`) and its best current offer for that method comes off the items, as amazon.in's "10% Instant Discount up to ₹1,500 on HDFC Bank EMI" does: once the items come to the offer's `minSpendMinor` after coupons, quantity discounts and a promotion code, `percentOff` of each unit (rounded down), all scaled down together to stay within `maxOffMinor`. Like the other discounts it goes into each item's `unitDiscountMinor` (its part `unitBankMinor`, the totals' `bankOfferMinor`), so cancellations and returns refund what was paid. Free delivery still goes by the items before it; tax by what's paid. EMI's minimum order is before it too. The order keeps `bank` (also in its `paymentLabel`, e.g. `EMI · 6 months · HDFC Bank`) whether or not an offer applied; other methods ignore it. A bank longer than 40 characters is `422 invalid_input` (`detail` `bank`).
- **Exchange offers:** in a store with them (amazon.in), Buy Now of one phone or laptop can trade an old one in: send `buyNow.exchange: {deviceId, condition}`, a model from `GET /exchange-devices` of the kind the product's category takes (`GET /products/:id`'s `exchange.kind`) and `good` (switches on, screen undamaged) or `screen_damaged` (switches on, screen cracked or marked). It takes the device's value off, half that with a damaged screen, never more than half the product's price, after every other discount and never more than what's left of the item. Like them it goes into the item's `unitDiscountMinor` (its part `unitExchangeMinor`, the totals' `exchangeMinor`), so cancelling refunds what was paid; free delivery and EMI's minimum go by the price before it, tax by what's paid. The order keeps `exchange: {deviceId?, device, condition, valueMinor}` and the old device is collected when the order is delivered. A product whose category takes no exchange, or a device that isn't listed (or is of another kind or store), is `409 exchange_unavailable` (`detail` `product` or `device`), and so is an exchange with `qty` other than 1 (`detail` `qty`) or from the cart (`detail` `cart`); a malformed `buyNow.exchange` is `422 invalid_input` (`detail` `buyNow.exchange`).
- **GST invoices** (India): send `gst: {gstin, name}` to have the invoice made out to a business, as amazon.in's "Use GST invoice" does, so it can claim input tax credit. The GSTIN is 15 characters (state code 01–38 or 97, PAN, entity number, `Z`, check character) and must check out; spaces are dropped and it's upper-cased. The business name is 1–100 characters. Otherwise `422 invalid_input` with `detail` `gstin` or `gstName`, before anything is reserved; in the US store, `409 gst_unavailable`. A blank `gstin` means none. The order comes back with `gst`, and the invoice is billed to that business. `PATCH /orders/:id` changes or removes it until the order ships.
- **Sizes:** each item of a product that comes in sizes is ordered in the size its cart line (or `buyNow.size`) has, as the item's `size`; a line without one of the product's sizes fails the order with `422 size_required` (`detail` the product's id) and nothing is placed. Cancellations and returns show the size with the item.
- **Purchase limits:** a product with `maxPerCustomer` can be bought that many times over, all told, by one account: the units in its orders that aren't cancelled (returned ones still count, as on Amazon, and items cancelled from an order don't). An order that would go past it fails with `409 purchase_limit`, `detail` the product's id, Buy Now and the cart alike, and nothing is placed. The product page, cart and checkout say the limit and how many more you can buy, and cap the quantity at it.
- **Delivery instructions:** `shipping.instructions` (up to 250 characters) goes on the order as `shipTo.instructions`. It's copied like the rest of the address, so editing a saved address later doesn't change orders already placed.
- **Drop-off spot:** `shipping.dropoff` is where to leave the package when nobody's there to take it: `front_door`, `back_door`, `side_porch`, `garage`, `mailroom`, `reception` (building reception) or `property_staff`; absent (or `""`) for no preference, anything else `422 invalid_input` (`detail` `dropoff`) before the order is placed. It goes on the order as `shipTo.dropoff` once the order exists (a pickup order takes none), and once the order is delivered the site says where it was left ("Left at the front door") instead of who it was handed to.

`Order` has these fields:
- Identity: `id` (`114-…` US / `402-…` IN), `market, currency`
- Status: `status: placed | awaiting_payment | cancelled`
- Payment: `paymentMethod, paymentLabel`, `prepaidAt?` for a Pay on Delivery order paid ahead with `POST /orders/:id/pay-now` (its method is then the one it was paid by), `emiMonths?` for an EMI order, and `split?: {balanceMinor, chargedMinor}` for one paid partly from the balance (see split payments above; `totals` is still the whole order)
- `exchange?: {deviceId?, device, condition, valueMinor}`: the old device traded in with Buy Now (see Exchange offers; `deviceId` goes when the store stops listing the model), its value in `totals.exchangeMinor` and the item's `unitExchangeMinor`
- Money: `totals`
- Delivery: `shipTo`, `shipSpeed?: fast | day` (absent means standard), `deliveryDay?` (the ISO weekday a `day` order goes to), `pickup?: {pointId, code}` for a pickup order (see Pickup), `deliveryOtp?` (six digits) for a high-value order the courier hands over only for its one-time password (see Delivery OTP), `releaseAt?` for a pre-order (see Pre-orders), and `gift?: {message?, wrapped?}` for a gift order
- `archivedAt?`: when you archived it (absent while it isn't)
- Lines: `items: [{productId, title, image, seller, unitPriceMinor, qty, unitDiscountMinor?, unitMemberMinor?, unitQtyDiscountMinor?, unitPromoMinor?, unitBankMinor?, unitExchangeMinor?, unitGuaranteeMinor?, protectionMinor?, size?, offerOf?, condition?, subscriptionId?, unitSnsMinor?, returnDays?, replacementOnly?}]` (`returnDays` is the item's category window when it was ordered, kept if the category's changes later: 0 when it can't be returned, absent for the store's, and the Renewed Guarantee's for an item bought renewed in a store with one (90 days in the US, or its category's if longer); `replacementOnly: true` likewise when its category was replacement only; `subscriptionId` and `unitSnsMinor` are set on an item a Subscribe & Save subscription delivered: the subscription, and its discount's part of `unitDiscountMinor`, added up in `totals` as `snsMinor?`; `offerOf` and `condition` are set on an item bought from another seller's offer: the product it's an offer on, and its condition when renewed or used; `unitMemberMinor`, `unitQtyDiscountMinor` and `unitPromoMinor` are the Plus exclusive deal's (with a Lightning Deal's early access), the quantity discount's and the promotion code's parts of `unitDiscountMinor`, and `totals` adds them up as `memberMinor?`, `qtyDiscountMinor?` and `promoMinor?`; `protectionMinor` is the protection plan bought with it, per unit; `size` is the size ordered, for a product that comes in sizes)
- `promoCode?`: the promotion code it was ordered with
- `gst?: {gstin, name}`: India, the business a GST invoice is made out to
- Timestamps: `createdAt, placedAt?, cancelledAt?`
- Delivery schedule (set once placed): `shippedAt?, outForDeliveryAt?, deliveredAt?`. Orders move along on their own: the stage is the latest of these that has passed (`preparing` before `shippedAt`). Admins can move them forward.
- Cancellation: `cancelReason?: customer | admin | sold_out | intercepted` (`intercepted`: stopped on its way at the shopper's request), and for orders that were placed or charged `refund?: {status, amountMinor, refundedAt?, balanceMinor?}` (`balanceMinor`: a split order's part of it back on the balance). `status` is `pending` / `succeeded` / `failed` for card refunds on Stripe, `succeeded` straight away for the simulated methods (the store balance is credited back), and `not_charged` for pay on delivery.
- `cancellations?`: items cancelled before it shipped while the rest kept coming, oldest first: `[{id, items, priceGuarantee?, itemsMinor, taxMinor, wrapMinor?, protectionMinor?, refund: {status, amountMinor, refundedAt?}, createdAt}]` (absent when there are none). `refund` works like the order's. An entry with `priceGuarantee: {productId, title, priceMinor, qty}` and no `items` is a Pre-order Price Guarantee refund (see Pre-orders): the item whose price dropped, the lower price per unit and how many.

### Returns

Delivered items can be returned within the store's window (`markets.return_days`). A return covers some or all of an order's lines and quantities; an order can have several returns, until every unit is in one that is open or received.

For a store-fault reason the shopper can ask for a replacement instead of a refund (`resolution: "replacement"`), as Amazon's "Replace item" does. The same items ship at once on an order-style schedule (`replacement.shippedAt`, `replacement.deliveredAt`), out of stock, at no charge; nothing is refunded, and the originals still have to be dropped off with the code. Each unit can be replaced once, inside the order's window, and only while it's on sale and in stock. Replaced units stay returnable for a refund, since the shopper has the new ones, and a replacement gets its own window from the day it's delivered: once the order's window has closed, the replacement units can still be returned (for a refund) until theirs does.

An item that comes in sizes can be exchanged for another size when it's `too_small` or `too_large`, as Amazon's "Exchange item" does: a replacement whose items each carry the `size` to send (`items: [{productId, qty, size}]`), one the product comes in other than the one ordered. It's a replacement in every other way: the new size ships at once, out of stock, at no charge, the originals go back with the code, and each unit can be swapped once. A store-fault replacement can name a different size too, or leave it out for the same one. The return's items show the new size as `exchangeSize`.

A return goes back one of two ways, as Amazon's "How would you like to return it?" asks: dropped off at one of the store's pickup points (a Hub Counter, or a Hub Locker, where the code is entered on the screen), either the one the shopper picks (`dropoffPoint`) or any, by `dropoffBy`; or picked up by a courier from the delivery address on a day the shopper picks (`pickupOn`, from the store's tomorrow until the day of `dropoffBy`). Either way it carries `dropoffCode`. Every return starts as a drop-off anywhere; the shopper can choose or change how it goes back with `PUT /returns/:id/method` until the items reach the store. An order collected from a pickup point has no address to collect from, so it can only be dropped off.

A refund can go onto the shopper's balance in the store instead of back to how they paid (`refundTo: "balance"`), as Amazon's "Refund to gift card balance" (amazon.in: the Amazon Pay balance) does. It's paid in as soon as the return is received, with nothing asked of the bank or Stripe. Orders paid from the balance are refunded to it anyway, and a replacement refunds nothing, so for those it stays `original`.

`reason` is one of `no_longer_needed`, `bought_by_mistake`, `better_price`, `too_small`, `too_large`, `damaged`, `defective`, `wrong_item`, `missing_parts`, `not_as_described`. The refund is priced when the return starts:
- `itemsMinor`: the returned units at the prices paid.
- `taxMinor`: the items' share of the order's tax (US). The return that brings the order to fully returned gets whatever tax is left, so the shares add up to the tax charged.
- `shipMinor`: the items' share of the delivery charge, but only for store-fault reasons.
- `protectionMinor?`: the returned units' protection plans. Returning an item cancels its plan, so the plan is refunded with it (absent without one).
- `wrapMinor?`: gift wrap, refunded only when the package didn't arrive.
- `refundMinor = itemsMinor + taxMinor + shipMinor + protectionMinor + wrapMinor`.
- A replacement is all zeros. A missing package refunds the whole order: what's left of `totals` after any cancelled items (or nothing, when it was sent again).

`Return` has these fields:
- `id, orderId, status: requested | received | rejected | cancelled, reason, comment?`
- `resolution: refund | replacement`, and for a replacement `replacement: {shippedAt, deliveredAt}`
- `items: [{productId, title, image, unitPriceMinor, qty, size?, exchangeSize?}]` (`size` is the size ordered, for a product that comes in sizes; `exchangeSize` the one sent instead, on an exchange)
- `itemsMinor, taxMinor, shipMinor, refundMinor`
- `refund?: {status: pending | succeeded | failed, refundedAt?}`, set once received
- `refundToBalance?: true` when the shopper asked for the refund on their balance in the store
- `balanceRefundMinor?`: on a split order, the part of the refund back on the balance (the rest goes back to how they paid)
- `dropoffCode` (e.g. `7F3A-09BC`, shown at a drop-off point or to the courier), `dropoffBy` (14 days after the start)
- `dropoffPoint?: PickupPoint` (`{id, kind, name, line1, city, state, postcode, hours, holdDays}`), the point chosen to drop it off at; `pickupOn?` (`YYYY-MM-DD`), the day a courier collects it. Neither: dropped off at any point.
- `rejectNote?`, `createdAt, receivedAt?, rejectedAt?, cancelledAt?`

When an admin marks a return received, the units go back into stock and the shopper is refunded (a replacement's refund is zero, recorded as `succeeded` at once): card payments on Stripe (a partial refund of the PaymentIntent, `metadata.returnId` set), simulated methods at once, pay on delivery at once to the shopper's bank (simulated), and a refund asked for on the balance at once to the balance (as are orders paid from it).

### A-to-z Guarantee claims

When something bought from one of the store's other sellers (not Amazon.com / Amazon.in itself) didn't arrive or isn't as described, and the seller hasn't put it right, the shopper can ask the store to step in, as Amazon's A-to-z Guarantee does. A claim is about one seller's items in one order. It can be filed once the order is delivered, for 90 days, and only after the shopper has contacted that seller about the order (`POST /me/support` with `seller` and `orderId`) at least 2 days before. While it's `under_review` the shopper can withdraw it (and file it again later). An admin grants it, refunding at once what's left of that seller's items in the order (the units at the prices paid, their protection plans and their share of tax and delivery) as a received return with reason `atoz_claim`, with nothing sent back; or denies it with a note the shopper sees. Either way it's in the shopper's messages.

| GET | `/orders/:id/claims` | | `{claims: [Claim]}`, oldest first. `404` for someone else's order. |
| POST | `/orders/:id/claims` | `{seller, reason, details}` | `201 {claim}`. `reason`: `not_received` or `not_as_described`; `details` 10–2,000 characters. `409 claim_not_allowed` with `detail` `sold_by_amazon`, `not_delivered`, `window_closed`, `cash_on_delivery` (`not_received` on an order paid on delivery), `already_claimed` (one under review or decided for that seller), `contact_seller_first`, `wait_for_seller` (contacted less than 2 days ago) or `nothing_left` (all of it returned or refunded). `422 invalid_input` with `detail` `seller` (not in the order), `reason` or `details`. |

### How card payment is confirmed

The customer never tells us they paid. Stripe does:

1. Stripe redirects to `/checkout/success?session_id=…` (web), and it also calls
   `POST /webhooks/stripe`. Both re-fetch the session from Stripe's API.
2. If Stripe says `paid`, `confirm_order_payment` runs. That function is only
   granted to the service role and checks that the amount and currency equal the
   order total. It is idempotent: the success page and the webhook may both run it.
   The order moves to `placed` and the ordered items leave the cart.
3. `checkout.session.expired` / `async_payment_failed`, `POST /orders/:id/cancel`,
   or the web cancel page all call `cancel_order`, which returns the reserved
   stock.

`POST /webhooks/stripe` verifies the `Stripe-Signature` header against
`STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`:
- It returns `503` when the secret is unset and `400` for a bad signature.
- Final domain outcomes (e.g. `payment_incomplete` for a forged "paid" event) are
  acknowledged with `200 {received, outcome}` so Stripe stops retrying.
- A gift card purchase's session (`metadata.kind: "gift_card"`) issues its code when paid; expiring, it has nothing to release.
- A Pay now session (`metadata.kind: "pay_now"`, a Pay on Delivery order paid by card before it arrives) makes the order a card order when paid (`confirm_pay_now_payment`, also run by the return trip to `/orders/:id/paid`), or refunds the payment when the order can no longer take it; expiring, the order simply stays on Pay on Delivery.
- An add-card session (`mode: "setup"`, from `/me/cards`) is acknowledged and nothing else: nothing is paid or held.
- `refund.created`, `refund.updated` and `refund.failed` settle a return's refund
  (matched by the refund's `metadata.returnId`), cancelled items' (by
  `metadata.cancellationId`) or a cancelled order's (by `metadata.orderId`, else
  its PaymentIntent). Enable these events on the Stripe
  endpoint alongside the `checkout.session.*` ones.

A payment that arrives after the order's reserved stock was released and sold
(`409 stock_released`) leaves the order cancelled (`cancelReason: sold_out`) and
refunds the card in full.

## AI layer

Four decision features, plus the product page's question box (`POST /products/:id/ask`) and search by image (`POST /searches/image`, where Gemini looks at the photo). Each one runs deterministic **rules** first, so every endpoint works with no configuration. When `GEMINI_API_KEY` is set, the server also asks Gemini (`GEMINI_MODEL`, default `gemini-2.5-flash`, called over plain REST).

How AI replies are handled:
- Replies must be JSON. They are validated with zod, and weights are clamped to the category's attribute keys (0..5).
- Any failure falls back to the rules result: no key, HTTP or safety error, timeout (4s for query parsing, 12s for a photo, 8s otherwise), unparseable JSON, or a schema mismatch.
- Every result carries `source: "rules" | "ai"`.
- Successful AI results are cached in `ai_cache` (service role only). TTLs: query 7d, profile 30d, reviews 14d, compare 1d, product questions 7d, photos 30d (keyed by the photo's hash).
- The key never leaves the server.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GET | `/ai/status` | | `{provider: "gemini:<model>" \| null, enabled}` |
| POST | `/ai/parse-query` | `{q}` | `{query: {keywords, category, budgetMinor, use, intents: [{kind: category\|budget\|use\|keyword, label, param, value, removable}], title, source}}`. `category` is one of the store's slugs. `use` is a preset id. Each intent's `param` is the `/s` search param it maps to (`dept`, `budget`, `use`, `k`). |

`ProductInput` is `{title, brand?, category, image, priceMinor, listMinor?, deal, couponPct?, maxPerCustomer?, sizes?, climate?, unit?, qtyDiscount?, memberPct?, releaseAt?, badge?, boughtPastMonth?, seller, shipsFrom, bullets: string[], description?, details?: [label, value][], stock, gallery?: string[], variantGroup?, variantAxis?, variantLabel?}`:
- `category` must be a slug this store carries (`422 invalid_category`).
- `image` is a site path (`/products/…`) or an `https://` URL. The admin pages upload files to the public `product-images` Storage bucket (JPEG, PNG or WebP, up to 3 MB) and store that URL.
- `listMinor` is the "was" price and must be above `priceMinor`. The discount % is worked out from it. `deal: true` (Today's Deals) needs a list price.
- `couponPct`: the product's coupon, a whole percent from 5 to 50; null or left out on create: none. Changing it keeps shoppers' coupons applied at the new percent; null removes the coupon and takes it off their carts.
- `maxPerCustomer`: "Limit N per customer", a whole number from 1 to 99; null or left out on create: no limit. Lowering it doesn't touch orders already placed, but shoppers who've bought that many can't buy more.
- `sizes`: the sizes it comes in, in size-chart order: 1 to 20 different sizes, each up to 12 characters (`["S", "M", "L"]`, `["UK 6", "UK 7"]`); null or left out on create: it doesn't come in sizes. Shoppers pick one before it goes in the cart. Cart lines in a size it no longer comes in need a new one before checkout; orders keep the size they were placed in.
- `climate`: its Climate Pledge Friendly certifications (see `Product.climate`), each once; `[]` for none, which a new product starts with. Left out on an update: they stay as they are. Anything else is `422 invalid_input` (`detail: "climate"`).
- `unit`: how much it holds, `{qty, kind}` as on `Product.unit` (`qty` above 0 and up to 100,000, rounded to 2 decimals; `422 invalid_input`, `detail: "unit"`, otherwise); null or left out on create: none, and no unit price. The admin form takes it as text, like `3 fl oz`, `150 ml` or `30 count`.
- `qtyDiscount`: "Save N% when you buy M or more", `{percentOff, minQty}` as on `Product.qtyDiscount` (`percentOff` a whole percent from 1 to 50, `minQty` a whole number from 2 to 99; `422 invalid_input`, `detail: "qtyDiscount"`, otherwise); null or left out on create: none. Changing it reprices carts at once but not orders already placed.
- `memberPct`: its Plus exclusive deal, the percent off a member pays (see `Product.memberPct`), a whole percent from 1 to 50 (`422 invalid_input`, `detail: "memberPct"`, otherwise); null removes it, and left out it stays as it is (none on create). Changing it reprices members' carts at once but not orders already placed.
- `releaseAt`: the release date of a product not out yet, an ISO timestamp with its offset (`422 invalid_input`, `detail: "releaseAt"`, otherwise); the web form takes a day and sends that day's midnight in the store. Null or left out on create: none. Moving it moves the pre-orders waiting on it (see *Pre-orders*)
- `bullets`: up to 10, each up to 300 characters.
- `description`: the product page's "Product description", up to 2,000 characters (blank or left out: none).
- `details`: the "Product information" table, up to 20 `[label, value]` rows (labels up to 40 characters, values up to 200). Left out on create: empty. The admin form edits it as one `Label: value` per line.
- `gallery`: up to 8 more images after `image`, each a site path or `https://` URL, shown in this order. Repeats and the main image are dropped. Left out on create: none.
- `variantGroup`: products of a store with the same group show as options of each other (each keeps its own price, stock, reviews and orders). A lowercase slug (`sony-wh-ch520`), up to 60 characters; blank or null for none, which also clears `variantAxis` and `variantLabel`.
- `variantAxis`: what the options differ by, e.g. `Color` or `Size` (up to 30 characters, `Style` when left out). Every product in a group uses the same one (`422 invalid_input`, `detail: "variantAxis"`).
- `variantLabel`: this product's option, e.g. `Black` (up to 60 characters). Needed with a group, and unique within it, ignoring case (`422 invalid_input`, `detail: "variantLabel"`).
- Validation errors are `422 invalid_input` with the field in `detail`.

Saving re-derives the product's rules insight (scores, pros and cons for its category's attributes). This happens on create, and on any change of category, title, brand or bullets. A new category replaces an AI insight too, since its attributes belong to the old category. New wording only replaces a rules insight.

## Walkthrough

```bash
API=http://localhost:3000/api/v1

# browse
curl "$API/products?q=headphones&sort=price-asc&market=US"

# guest cart: first write mints the token (X-Cart-Token response header)
curl -i -X POST "$API/cart/items" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"productId":"51rpbVmi9XL","qty":2}'

# account + token
curl -X POST "$API/auth/signup" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"email":"you@example.com","password":"a-long-password","name":"Your Name"}'
TOKEN=$(curl -s -X POST "$API/auth/token" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"email":"you@example.com","password":"a-long-password"}' | jq -r .accessToken)

# bring the guest cart along, then check out
curl -X POST "$API/cart/merge" -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H "X-Cart-Token: <token>"
curl -X POST "$API/orders" -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{
  "paymentMethod": "giftcard",
  "shipping": {"fullName":"Your Name","phone":"2065550123","line1":"410 Terry Ave N",
               "city":"Seattle","state":"WA","postcode":"98109"}}'
```

About the tables and functions:
- **Browser-facing roles cannot write any table directly.** The anon and authenticated roles either go through RLS-scoped policies or call functions with explicit grants. Order and total columns are never client-writable, and price and stock only by admins (`public.admins`), through the `products` policies.
- **Service-role-only functions.** `confirm_order_payment`, `attach_checkout_session`, `release_checkout_session`, `record_payment_intent`, `record_refund`, `record_return_refund`, `mark_sold_out`, `attach_gift_card_session`, `confirm_gift_card_purchase` and `email_in_use` are called by the server with `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY`.
- **Guest carts.** They are only reachable through the `cart_*` functions with their token. `purge_stale_guest_carts()` deletes guest carts that have been idle for 30 days. It is service-role only, so schedule it with pg_cron or call it from a cron job.

Tests: `npm run test:db` runs `test/integration/*` against the local stack. It covers:
- totals, carts, stock reservation and overselling, card confirmation rules
- RLS isolation, reviews, addresses, search and home content
- returns: the delivery and window checks, what's left to return, refund pricing (tax shares that add up, delivery only for store-fault reasons), cancel, admin receive (stock back, refund per payment method) and reject, the admin list per store, an order's returns for the admin order page, and real Stripe test-mode partial refunds
- review moderation: auto-hide on the third report, who sees a hidden review, the rating rollup, keep resolving reports, admin hide, the queue per store, admin-only access, delete
- ratings by feature: saved with a review and averaged over the visible ones (a hidden review stops counting), kept on a rewrite that leaves them out, cleared with null, only 1–5 stars on known features (and the table holds to that shape), served with the reviews and taken by POST
- account settings: sign-up, rename, email change (current password, taken addresses), password change (sessions ended, new session returned, reset-link sessions)
- the signed Stripe webhook
- product variants and galleries: sibling options per store in label order, unique labels (and the index behind them), one option name per group, archived options, leaving a group, the gallery cap; listings and ranked search showing one card per group, group counts and brand facets, and the swatch summaries
- search suggestions: completions, departments that follow the top completion, one card per group, store isolation, short and punctuation-only input
- related searches: only searches that find something are counted, the three-search threshold, shared-word ranking, reordered words and store isolation
- recommendations: a row of what was viewed with each product viewed lately, none started from recommended from another, the products asked not to be used left out, junk ids ignored, and nothing the caller bought recommended
- missing packages: the whole-order refund, the 30-day window, one report per order, cash on delivery, and sending it again (stock out now, nothing refunded, no second replacement, still returnable for a refund, refused without taking stock when sold out)
- replacement return windows: nothing changes until the replacement arrives, then its window runs from that day; after the order's window only the replacement units can go back, for a refund and never another swap, and both windows closing shuts the order
- unit price: an admin sets, changes and clears what a product holds (rounded to 2 decimals), shoppers read it off the catalog and search, an unknown unit is refused, the database refuses half a unit, and only admins write it
- product recalls: only admins recall, the hazard and remedy checked in the database, the product off sale for good (restoring it is refused), recalling again keeps the date, public per-store reads without the issuer, no direct writes, and the buyer (only) told on `/recalls` and in their messages
- lower prices: one open report per shopper and product (sent again, rewritten), only lower than the price with delivery, a real page or a recent day, checked in the database too, no direct writes, shoppers seeing only their own, the admin queue per store (lowest first), admin-only review, a new report after review, and the API
- pickup points: anyone lists a store's points and nobody writes them; a pickup order ships to the point with the shopper's name and phone and a six-digit code, on the standard schedule, takes no instructions, and stops being a pickup when sent to a saved address; the database refuses another store's point or an unknown one; lockers refuse cash on delivery while counters take it
- Delivery Day: only members pick a day, a weekday 1–7, and only through `set_delivery_day()`; a `day` order is refused without one, without Plus and in the India store; it arrives on the chosen weekday at 9:00/11:30 store time, no sooner than standard and within a week of it, free for members, on the schedule `plannedSchedule()` predicts; leaving Plus drops the day while placed orders keep theirs
- Plus plans: joining on a plan the store sells (3 months refused in the US store), the period's end a month, 3 months or a year on; switching from the next renewal (and back), only to plans the store sells; renewal off and on; members only and no direct writes; the renewal run ending a membership whose renewal is off once its period is over, and renewing one that's due on the plan it switched to, once per period it's behind
- Pay Later: the store's limit and method (India only), activating (again changing nothing, refused in the US), refused orders before activating, over the available limit and while a bill is overdue, the order's label, a cancelled order's refund freeing the limit, a return's refund to Pay Later (and never to the balance), the bill (bought before the 1st, due on the 5th) and repayments against it, refused repayments (more than owed, nothing, other methods), and no direct writes
- Mobile recharge: the plans (India only), refused numbers, circles, plans and methods, a recharge paid from the balance (and refused when the balance doesn't cover it), by UPI and by net banking, the cashback on the balance (capped at ₹25), the transactions, and no direct writes
- Climate Pledge Friendly: the search filter and its count, the certifications on a product and an offer of it, admins setting and clearing them, and unknown certifications refused
- small businesses: the seeded brands found by the search filter and counted, an admin marking a brand (the badge on its products and an offer of one), changing its story and removing it, brands the store doesn't sell and duplicates refused, and shoppers unable to write
- drop-off spots: saved on an address and placed on its order, changed and cleared on the order until it's out for delivery, copied with the address when the order moves, unknown spots and pickup orders refused
- Bill payments: the billers (India only), a fetched bill (the same every time) and its payment in full from the balance, a bill paid twice or for a changed amount, refused billers, accounts, amounts and methods, a FASTag account tidied and paid by net banking, a balance that doesn't cover it, the transactions, and no direct writes
- Plus Household: inviting (members with their own Plus only; not their own email, re-inviting replaces the invite, not once someone's joined), the invite seen only by that email, accepting (free delivery from then on, `/me/plus` shared; refused with their own Plus or for someone else's invite), the member's plan and Delivery Day refused, declining, stopping, leaving, sharing ending with the membership and on joining Plus, and no direct writes.
- quantity discounts: an admin sets and clears one, an out-of-range one is refused and the database refuses half of one, nothing comes off below the quantity, the percent comes off after a clipped coupon in Buy Now quotes and the cart, a promotion's minimum spend counts after it and the promotion goes last, and ordered items keep it so a cancelled line refunds what was paid
- Plus exclusive deals: an admin sets one, a shopper who isn't a member pays the full price, a Plus member's quote and order take the member price off first (stored per item, the order total matching the quote) with a coupon on what's left, and an out-of-range percent is refused
- bought together: pairs from placed orders only, the two-shopper threshold, sold-out products left out, and the product page's pick (order pairs first, then accessories)
- list item details: comment, quantity and priority set by the owner only (not by direct writes or other shoppers), checked in the database, kept on a move, shown on the shared list, and the API route
- needs and has: givers split an item's quantity, more than is still needed refused with the number left, a covered item last on the list and `someone` to others, nothing shown to the owner, a plain mark taking all that's left, and undo freeing a giver's share
- also bought: purchases across a shopper's placed orders (not only the same one), cancelled orders left out, the two-shopper threshold, sold-out products left out, and the API route
- admin catalog: product writes (including the description and spec table), archiving (listings, carts, checkout, saved lists), insights on save, and categories (create, rename, store navs, reorder, delete guards)
- buy again: one entry per product across orders, newest first, cancelled orders left out, per store and per shopper, sold-out and archived products last, and the API route
- brand follows: following a brand on sale (trimmed, again keeps the first date), refused for a brand with nothing on sale in that store, per store and per shopper, unfollowing, signed-out callers refused, the newest products of each brand followed
- communication preferences: everything on to start, turning kinds off and back on (in a fixed order, again changing nothing), anything else refused, written only through the function, signed-out callers refused, turned-off review requests and answers left out of the inbox (and back once on), and `/me/message-preferences`
- contact seller: who counts as a store seller, orders narrowed to the seller's items, a case opened with the seller (and the order) that the admins answer for them, the shopper's inbox naming the seller, unknown sellers and orders without their items turned away, and the database's own checks
- GST invoices: checkout adds the tidied details, the database checks the GSTIN's check character and the name, both or neither, change and removal until shipping (`gst_locked` after), the owner only, and India only
- order lifecycle: the saved schedule in both time zones, shopper and admin cancel windows, stock and refund state per payment method, admin moves and listing, refund bookkeeping, and real Stripe test-mode refunds
- Lightning Deals: going live sets the deal price against the price before; units ordered at it are claimed, and the last one ends the deal and puts the price back; it ends at its time, when the product goes off sale or runs out (a due deal on one never goes live), and when an admin reprices it (their price stands); prices at or above the product's, offers and overlapping windows refused; public reads, no direct writes, the runs for the service role only, and planning two a store an hour ahead
- Pre-order Price Guarantee: an admin's lower price before the end of the release day lowers a placed pre-order's items and total and refunds the difference (to the balance at once, on Stripe for a card), in messages and transactions; a rise or a smaller drop pays nothing, a bigger one only the rest; an unpaid order, the day after release and an order placed after release get nothing
- Lightning Deal early access: half an hour before its start a member pays the deal price (as a member saving, the product keeping its price) and anyone else the full price, with nothing claimed; not before then; a member's units are claimed, and the deal can sell out before it starts, after which it's the full price
- Admin Lightning Deals: admins schedule a deal (live at once when it starts now, upcoming otherwise) and cancel one (a live one's price goes back); shoppers and guests can't; refusals for prices, units over stock, past starts, overlaps and over 12 hours; listed live, upcoming and ended, with the API in this store only
- Deal watches: a shopper watches an upcoming deal and stops; a live, ended or unknown one can't be watched; guests can't; each sees only their own; a watched deal going live is in their messages (`over` once it ends), in its own store only; the API's `watching` flags
- Bank Offers: anyone reads a store's offers and nobody else writes them; net banking and EMI with a bank take its best offer off each unit (scaled under the cap, nothing under the minimum or for another bank or method), the order keeps the bank and its label says so, card and other methods ignore it, the totals and invoice show it apart, and a cancellation refunds what was paid
- exchange offers: a Buy Now of one phone with an old phone traded in takes its value off (half with a damaged screen, at most half the price), the order keeps the device and its condition, and cancelling refunds what was paid; refused for a category without them, a device of another kind, a quantity over 1 and a cart order
- Pay on Delivery ceiling: India's ceiling is ₹50,000 and the US has none; a Buy Now of exactly ₹50,000 is taken on Pay on Delivery, one over it is refused (and placed by UPI), and a cart over it is refused with the cart kept.
- delivery OTP: an India order of ₹10,000 or more gets a six-digit code, a cheaper one and a pickup order don't, and a pickup order sent to an address gets one.
- Pay now: a Pay on Delivery order paid by UPI, net banking (with its bank) or the balance (refused when short) takes that method and `prepaidAt`, and cancelling it refunds it (the balance back); paying twice, a delivered or cancelled order, someone else's and another method are refused, and the API route. By card: only the server opens and confirms a session; a paid one makes it a card order once (again is a no-op), and a second session, or one for an order cancelled, delivered or repriced since, is refunded; with Stripe, one page per order, closed when it's cancelled
- A-to-z Guarantee claims: a claim needs the seller contacted about the order 2 days before, a delivered order inside 90 days, a seller other than the store and something left; one per seller until withdrawn; shoppers see only their own and can't decide them; an admin grant refunds what's left of that seller's items as a received `atoz_claim` return (and blocks returning them again), a deny needs a note; both are in the shopper's messages; the API's routes and refusals
- return windows by category: only admins set one, in a store that lists it, from 0 to 365 days; shoppers read the category's or the store's; an order line keeps the window it was ordered with (a later change is for new orders, and shoppers can't write it); each line goes back within its own window, a not-returnable one never; and the admin and product API routes
- replacement-only categories: only admins set it; an order line keeps it (a later change is for new orders, and shoppers can't write it); a change of mind or a refund while it can be replaced is refused, alone or beside a refundable item; a faulty one is replaced, and refunded when sold out; other items go back as before; and the admin and product API routes
- size exchanges: a too-small item swapped for a size it comes in (stock out of the product, nothing refunded, the new size on the return), and refused without a size, with the one ordered, one it doesn't come in, or a size on a refund; a too-large refund keeps the delivery charge; and the API route
- fit from returns: a sized product with no size returns says nothing; too-small returns (an exchange among them) make it run small, a too-large one beside them doesn't tip it, enough too-large ones even it out; and the product API route
- typical price: a new product's price is recorded, a repricing adds to it and a stock change doesn't; shoppers can't read the history; under a week of prices gives none; a price cut below the median shows it, a list price above the price hides it; and the product API route
- Trade-In: a US model quotes its value, half with a damaged screen, and an India one or an unknown condition is refused; 5 open is the limit; cancelling works once; another shopper sees and cancels none; only admins list, receive or send back, in the trade-in's store; a device that came in worse pays its value then, a better one no more than the quote, onto the balance; the page and API routes
- Renewed Guarantee: a US line bought renewed keeps 90 days and goes back 45 days after delivery while a new one from the same order can't; an India renewed line keeps its category's window; `listRenewed` and `GET /renewed` list the store's renewed offers with their products, and only those in stock
- No-Rush Shipping: the US store's reward and the India store's refusal, the schedule 4 days after standard at standard's price, the reward kept on the order and not writable by shoppers, credited to the balance once it has shipped and only once, nothing for a cancelled order, settled only for its own shopper (not guests), and credited by checkout before paying from the balance
- two-step verification: setting up an authenticator app (a fresh key each time, the unfinished one dropped), a wrong code refused, the right one giving an `aal2` session; then a password-only token is refused by the database (`two_step_required`) and by the API (`401`), for that account only, until `/auth/token/verify` takes the code; turning it on and off through the API (off needs the password), after which the password is enough again
- holiday returns: a US order placed in December goes back until January 31 (`returnBy` past its 30 days), an India one doesn't, and the stores' `holiday_returns` match their config
- split payment: the balance part taken when a card order is placed and the card asked for the rest only, given back when the checkout is abandoned, refused when the balance covers the order or with a method that doesn't combine; refunds of cancelled items and of the order go to the card first and the rest to the balance, so it ends where it started; a UPI order placed at once, its returns refunded to UPI up to what it paid and then to the balance (succeeded at once)
- search by condition: new, renewed and used counted over the scope and each keeping only what can be bought that way (a product out of stock new but offered new by another seller, an offer out of stock or off sale not counting), the storefront's ranked search too, and More Buying Choices summing the offers on sale and in stock
